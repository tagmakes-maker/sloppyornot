# Community impact (for Devpost and the pitch)

Judging includes community building and support. This is the case, strongest first. Lead with what we can demo.

## 1. The room grades the room

Every HarborHack team gets reviewed by every other team. Scan a QR code, 20 seconds, swipe. The hackathon becomes its own feedback loop, live, on Sunday. This is the one we demonstrate.

## 2. Builders stop building alone

Gituationship turns a room of solo projects into collaborations. The agent finds where one project's weakness is another project's strength and proposes the pair. That is community formation, not just support.

## 3. Verified Build as a portable credential

The results page is shareable. A builder without the degree, resume, or connections gets a link that proves real humans used the thing: what was built, what was tested, what real users thought, what could improve, and who they could build with next. Useful to employers eventually. Useful to the builder today, because it is feedback, not just a score.

Pitch rule: SloppyOrNot grades the product, never the person. The credential is a side effect of honest product feedback. Say it that way or judges hear "hiring filter" and start thinking about bias.

## 4. The day-zero cohort

Products that opt in get tracked for six months on whether AI models start recommending them. First public dataset on how brand-new builds earn discovery. Research for the whole ecosystem, run by the ARO Index. SloppyOrNot score and ARO score stay separate numbers.

## 5. Free for hackathons, student showcases, and local dev communities

One line. Costs nothing to say. Schools, employers, nonprofits, meetups.

## The sentence

People leave a hackathon with more than a participation sticker: proof of their work, useful feedback, and potentially their next collaborator.

## What this does NOT add to the MVP

No new screens. The shareable Verified Build page is the results page we are already building, with a public link. The Product Profile already holds everything it needs.
